Methodology

How the engine decides

We do not re-rank your MT5 table. We look for plateaus, and tell you how much evidence backs them. These are the steps, in the order the engine runs them.

01

It reads your export as it is

Drop the XML that MT5 exports, with commas or dots as decimals. The engine does not trust column names to tell parameters from metrics: it uses how they behave (a parameter has the same value for the same pass in-sample and in forward; a metric does not). It does need to recognize the metric columns (profit, profit factor, drawdown, trades) by name, and it reads English and Spanish headers: if key columns are not recognized, it tells you instead of judging with less than it seems. If it drops any row, it tells you.

02

Your minimums before any ranking

A configuration only enters the search if it clears your floors in-sample: profit factor, drawdown, number of trades and positive profit. A ranking always produces a “top 5%”, even when everything loses money; a floor does not.

03

Quality without the Result column

Result is whatever you asked MT5 to optimize, so it is biased in its own favor. The engine measures quality with several objective metrics at once (profit factor, recovery, Sharpe, drawdown, trades), so a configuration does not win just by standing out in one of them.

04

Plateaus, not peaks

For each configuration it checks its neighbors, measured in steps of your grid (from 30 to 50, or from 0.1 to 0.2, is one step). A plateau is a connected zone where the neighbors also clear your minimums. Isolated peaks are rejected. Inside the plateau it picks the configuration with the best combined rank in-sample and forward, averaged with its neighbors: not the tallest point, and not luck at a single point. If there is no plateau and there is a forward, it gives a tentative suggestion by the same rule and says so. In our blind benchmark it beats picking the pass MT5 lists first (highest in-sample profit), the best forward row, the rank sum and random choice; it narrowly loses to “average with neighbors” when every case without a plateau counts as a miss (with the tentative suggestion, it wins).

05

The forward confirms or demotes

Plateaus are found with the in-sample only. Then the forward puts them to the test: your minimums are required again of the plateau’s configurations and their quality is compared. If many fail, the plateau drops in rank and you are warned. Your minimums are required in each period separately, so a good forward cannot hide a weak in-sample, or the other way round. Only to choose within an already validated plateau are the two periods’ ranks added together.

06

It estimates how much could be chance

Test thousands of configurations and some will look great by pure chance. The engine computes the best Sharpe you would expect from luck alone with that many trials and compares it with yours. If yours does not beat it, the report flags it as critical: the top result may be nothing but noise. Beating it is necessary, not sufficient: it rules out that the result comes only from trying a lot, but it does not validate the strategy.

07

It tests itself

What the engine requires to call something a plateau (minimum quality, robustness, support, size…) are our decisions, not laws. To check that the recommendation does not depend on them, the search is repeated 50 times moving them by up to ±20%, and it counts how often the same plateau still wins. The same is done, with fewer repetitions, moving your minimums (with very large files this second test is skipped, and you are told). If a recommendation only appears with the exact numbers, it is not a recommendation.

08

What each evidence level means

The verdict grades how much your data back the recommendation, not your EA. None of the levels means “go trade”.

Strong
A plateau with no warnings, confirmed by the forward and by an unseen period. The most your data can back.
Good
A plateau with no warnings, validated on the forward. It is the ceiling while the unseen period is missing: the forward was already used to choose.
Moderate
A plateau with real support, but with warnings worth reading, or one that the unseen period does not confirm.
Weak
At least one serious limitation: for example, no forward, no connected zone, or a best result that does not beat chance.
Insufficient
So few configurations clear your minimums that nothing can be said, for or against.
09

The acid test: your backtest on unseen data

The cleanest check is to test the configuration on a period you used neither to optimize nor to validate. Drop that backtest report into the app and it tells you whether the result is normal for your EA, with a Monte Carlo over your own trades; it warns you if there are too few trades to conclude anything, and shows what happens if spread or slippage get worse than in the optimization.

What it does not do

  • A plateau is stability in your sample, not a promise of future profit.
  • It does not tell you to buy, sell or fund an EA: evidence is not permission to trade.
  • It does not judge the EA’s logic, the quality of the price history or the spread and commission of your backtest: if the source backtest is optimistic, it stays optimistic here.
  • The forward is already used to validate and rank plateaus, so it is not a blind period: the cleanest check is an unseen period.
  • It does not compute classic PBO, Reality Check or SPA: MT5 exports do not include the equity curves those tests need.
  • With many parameters or a genetic search (not a full grid), neighborhood and plateau size are capped conservatively: it may fall short, never invent support, and you are told.

Why not just ask an AI?

Paste your table into a chat and you will get a convincing explanation of why the best result in the list is good. With thousands of configurations tested, some always look good by chance. That is not analysis — it is a story.

Read the full explanation
Same data, same answer
A language model writes every answer from scratch: it changes with the wording of your question, the order of the data and from one day to the next. Orometra is a calculation: same files, same result, today and a year from now. That is what lets you compare one optimization with another.
A fixed method, not an improvised one
Ask an AI to assess robustness and it makes up a method on the spot. You cannot know whether it is the same as last time, or whether it is right. Orometra's method is written on this page, it is always the same, and automated tests check that it keeps doing what it says.
Numbers you can check
Language models can give figures that sound right and come from no calculation. Even when they write code, you have to review that code every time. Every figure in Orometra's report comes from the columns of your export and can be traced back.
It knows when to say no
An AI is built to be helpful, so it tends to answer even when the data cannot support a conclusion. Orometra lowers the evidence level and says why: if there is no plateau, if the forward does not confirm it or if the result does not beat chance, it stays at weak; and if the data are not enough even for that, it says “insufficient”.
Your strategy stays yours
For an AI to analyze your optimization you have to upload your parameters — the heart of your strategy — to another company's servers. Orometra runs the analysis in your browser: your files never leave your computer.
Something you can use
An AI gives you text. Orometra gives you a specific configuration, its evidence level, the reasons, a .set to load in MT5 and the range to re-optimize around it.

AI is very good at explaining concepts: use it to understand the report and ask it anything about it. But the decision of which configuration you risk your money on deserves a criterion that is fixed, tested and does not change from one conversation to the next.
